            <h1>How to Make a Worm Bin 🪱</h1>
            <p>
                Creating a DIY worm bin is a simple and cost-effective way to start vermicomposting at home. With just a few materials, you can create a sustainable system that transforms food waste into nutrient-rich compost for your garden.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="section">
            <h2>Materials Needed 🛠️</h2>
            <ul>
                <li>Plastic tote (10-50 gallons)</li>
                <li>Power drill with drill bits</li>
                <li>Peat moss, coconut coir, or shredded newspaper (bedding)</li>
                <li>Red wiggler worms (<em>Eisenia fetida</em>)</li>
                <li>Food scraps (vegetable peels, coffee grounds, eggshells)</li>
                <li>Spray bottle (for moisture control)</li>
            </ul>
            <div class="image-container">
              
            </div>
        </div>

        <div class="section">
            <h2>Step 1: Preparing the Bin 📦</h2>
            <p>To create a proper environment for your worms, you need a well-ventilated bin.</p>

            <h3>Drill Drainage Holes</h3>
            <p>Flip the bin upside down and drill small holes on the bottom for drainage. If you are wondering how many holes you are over thinking this.</p>
            <div class="image-container">
                <img src="" alt="Drainage Holes">
            </div>

            <h3>Drill Ventilation Holes</h3>
            <p>On the upper sides of the bin, drill a row of small holes apart to allow airflow. Again if you are wondering how many holes, the spacing you are overthinking it. Just drill baby drill!</p>
            <div class="image-container">
                <img src="" alt="Ventilation Holes">
            </div>

            <h3>Drill Lid Holes (Optional)</h3>
            <p>If keeping the lid on, drill small holes along the top edge for extra ventilation.</p>
            <div class="image-container">
                <img src="" alt="Side Ventilation Holes">
            </div>
        </div>

        <div class="section">
            <h2>Step 2: Adding Bedding 🛏️</h2>
            <p>Worms need a soft, absorbent material to live in. You can use peat moss, shredded newspaper, coconut coir, or cardboard, or even a mixture of different bedding materials.</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Add a 3-4 inch layer of damp bedding to the bottom of the bin.</li>
                <li>Moisten it with a spray bottle. It should be damp but not dripping wet.</li>
                <li>Fluff it up to create space for airflow.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>

        <div class="section">
            <h2>Step 3: Adding the Worms 🪱</h2>
            <ul>
                <li>Use <strong>Red Wigglers (Eisenia fetida)</strong> as they are the best for composting.</li>
                <li>Start with <strong>½ - 1 pound of worms</strong> (about 500-1000 worms).</li>
                <li>Gently place them on top of the bedding—they will burrow down naturally.</li>
            </ul>
            </div>

            <div class="section">
                <h2>Step 4: Feeding Your Worms 🍎🥦</h2>
                <div class="card">
                    <h3>✅ Best Foods:</h3>
                    <ul>
                        <li>Vegetable peels</li>
                        <li>Coffee grounds & filters</li>
                        <li>Fruit scraps (no citrus)</li>
                        <li>Shredded cardboard & paper</li>
                        <li>Crushed eggshells</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
                
                <div class="card">
                    <h3>❌ Avoid:</h3>
                    <ul>
                        <li>Meat, dairy, and oily foods</li>
                        <li>Citrus fruits (too acidic)</li>
                        <li>Onion & garlic (worms dislike them)</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
    
                <div class="tips">
                    <strong>Pro Tip:</strong> Bury food scraps under bedding to reduce odors and fruit flies.
                </div>
            </div>
    
            <div class="section">
                <h2>Step 5: Maintaining the Bin 🔄</h2>
                <ul>
                    <li>Check moisture weekly (it should feel like a wrung-out sponge).</li>
                    <li>Turn bedding lightly to prevent anaerobic conditions.</li>
                    <li>Harvest compost every 2-3 months by moving finished compost to one side and adding fresh bedding/food on the other.</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
    
            <div class="section">
                <h2>Step 6: Harvesting the Vermicompost 🌱</h2>
                <ul>
                    <li>Move fresh food to one side of the bin.</li>
                    <li>Wait a few days—worms will migrate to the fresh food.</li>
                    <li>Scoop out the finished compost and use it in your garden or houseplants!</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
    
            <div class="section">
                <h2>Troubleshooting Tips 🛠️</h2>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>Bad smell?</strong> Too much food or too wet. Add dry bedding and reduce feeding.</li>
                    <li><strong>Worms escaping?</strong> Bin may be too wet or acidic. Adjust moisture and avoid citrus.</li>
                    <li><strong>Fruit flies?</strong> Always bury food scraps under bedding.</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
    
            <div class="section">
                <h2>Final Thoughts 💚</h2>
                <p>Vermicomposting is an easy, sustainable way to recycle food scraps and enrich soil naturally. With the right setup and a little maintenance, you’ll have healthy worms and nutrient-rich compost in no time!</p>
            </div>
